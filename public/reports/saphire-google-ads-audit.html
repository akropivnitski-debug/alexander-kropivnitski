02

Top risks flagged

1
Remarketing is effectively switched off account-wide. Every system-defined audience (all visitors, product viewers, cart abandoners, past buyers) is closed under a Restricted Targeting in Personalized Advertising policy citation, tied to the health-adjacent, children's-focus content the brand is built on. The only open segments are a generic Google-engaged list and an empty custom combination list with zero campaign usage. Full detail in Section 04 and Section 10.
2
Confirmed finding: Demand Gen shows persistently low attributed efficiency. $81,523 spent lifetime for $36,617 in attributed conversion value (0.45x attributed ROAS), and still at 0.27x in the last 30 days despite the team already cutting its budget share from 24.5% to 9.5%. Google Ads cannot confirm whether Demand Gen creates value outside what it measures. Detail in Section 08.
3
Confirmed finding: PMax attributed efficiency declined in July. Broken down by month (Section 02), PMax's attributed conversion value after ad spend climbed every month from March through June ($2,091 to $14,862). In July it dropped to $3,847 at 1.19x attributed ROAS on similar spend. PMax also now carries 39.0% of last-30-day spend, the largest share of any channel. The cause is not established from this data alone, timing alone does not prove causality. Several hypotheses, including the audience-signal restriction in Risk 1, are outlined for investigation in Section 07.

Google Ads flags the primary conversion action for an Enhanced Conversions setup issue. "Google Shopping App Purchase," the primary purchase action Smart Bidding optimizes against, is flagged by Google Ads for an Enhanced Conversions setup issue. Signal quality on automated bid strategies may be affected; this data does not show that every strategy is materially degraded. Detail in Section 04.
5
Amazon frequently appears in the same auctions as the Brand Search campaign and often ranks above it. 58.4% to 63.5% search overlap rate and a 90.98% to 93.00% top-of-page rate from amazon.com against the two Brand Search campaigns (period: Sep 1, 2025 to Jul 28, 2026), plus a cluster of "best saffron gummies" and "is it a scam" content sites appearing for comparison-stage searches. Auction Insights shows auction competition, not clicks won or lost. Full detail in the Competitive Intelligence Brief and Section 11.

03

Top opportunities found

1
Resolve the personalized advertising restriction, or build the strategy around it deliberately. Either the policy citation is fixable (content or categorization change) or it is permanent, in which case every PMax and Demand Gen recommendation here needs to lean on contextual and product-feed signals instead of audience signals. Confirming which one it is changes how both channels should be targeted going forward.
2
Reduce Demand Gen exposure and separate testing budgets, do not just trim. The team has already cut its share by more than half. The remaining spend still shows attributed ROAS below 1.00x. This may indicate that moving spend fully into Search or Shopping, or rebuilding Demand Gen from scratch around a different creative and targeting logic, improves attributed media efficiency at the same budget level. Search cannot be assumed to absorb all reallocated spend at its current efficiency, further validation is required.
3
Investigate scaling the DSA campaign after addressing its rank-constrained impression share. 7.24x attributed ROAS on only $6,161 of spend, the highest observed attributed ROAS in the account. Search lost impression share (Section 05) shows this campaign is rank-constrained, not budget-constrained. Further validation of rank drivers is required before scaling budget.
4
Review the Enhanced Conversions diagnostic details for the Purchase action. Confirm data quality and implementation ownership before changing bidding. Google Ads flags this action for a setup issue; the exact cause has not been confirmed from this data.
5
Stand up the Branding keywords switch as the account's first automation layer. An hourly auction-insights check that reacts to Amazon or affiliate encroachment on brand terms directly targets Risk 5, and gives the account its first piece of always-on monitoring at the same time.
6
Protect and extend what is already working. Both Brand campaigns (Search 5.66x lifetime, 4.80x last 30 days; Shopping 3.51x lifetime, 3.18x last 30 days) and the Kids Theme NB Search campaign are proven, scaled winners. They don't need fixing, they are the floor the rest of the account should be measured against.

Fundamentals

Competitive brief scan

Full detail lives in the Competitive Intelligence Brief, cited here rather than repeated. Two findings from that brief matter most:

  • Amazon resellers overlap up to 62% of Brand Search query traffic, with a 90%+ top-of-page rate.
  • Affiliate content sites ("best saffron gummies," comparison-stage searches) rank for queries where Saphire has no owned page competing.

Both are still visible in this account's own Auction Insights. Neither has been addressed since.

Audience analysis review

Full detail lives in the Targeted Audience Analysis. The persona, Sarah Whitfield, decides under guilt and time pressure: natural alternative vs. being pushed toward medication. That tension shows up twice elsewhere in this account.

  • Best-converting pages lead with it. The homepage and quiz funnel (Section 14) speak directly to that tension.
  • Underperforming pages don't. 5-reasons-gummies and the bare product page lead with product features instead.
  • Ad copy doesn't either. The RSA copy in Section 05 leans on generic supplement language, "Premium," "Organic," "Shop," not the fear-versus-relief language the persona actually responds to.

Business goals alignment

The account counts new customers but doesn't bid for them. That's a mismatch between the stated growth goal and how spend actually gets allocated.

  • Customer acquisition is the stated goal. The conversion flag is active, 27,016 weighted new customers tracked across the period.
  • New Customer Lifetime Value is $0.00 on every conversion action. Bid strategies don't weight for it.

Full detail in Section 07 and Section 09.

03

Account architecture

Naming taxonomy check

Campaign names carry real information: channel, brand or non-brand, angle, bid strategy history. But the delimiter convention isn't consistent, three examples, each bracketed and piped differently: [V] [Search] US | Brand | mCPC, [V] | [DG] | US | [PDP] | Batch 1//AD1, [V] [PMAX| AO] US | NB | Different LPs. That makes filtering or scripting against campaign names harder than it should be, which matters directly for the proposed Branding keywords switch. A one-time rename to a single fixed template (channel, brand flag, angle, bid strategy) would pay for itself the first time anyone automates against these names.

67 campaigns exist in the account's history.

  • 11 are Enabled.
  • 56 are Paused. 52 of those are Demand Gen batch tests: 10 Reasons ADHD, 7 Reasons ADHD, Natural Alternative, PDP, Quiz Funnel, Bed Wetting, each split into 8 to 11 numbered batches.

Losers were actually turned off rather than left to bleed, that's disciplined testing. But it's left a large graveyard sitting in the account. None of it spends, but it's real housekeeping debt for anyone reading this account cold.

Budget allocation logic and shared budgets audit

  • Enabled campaigns carry $3,656/day of combined budget cap, about $111,000 a month. Actual recent spend is roughly $56,000 a month, half the headroom sits unused at the account level. Specific campaigns (Section 13) are still individually losing impression share to budget.
  • Paused campaigns still carry $5,040.60/day of dead budget sitting on record. Harmless while paused, but worth clearing so nobody accidentally reactivates a $110 or $500 daily test campaign.
  • 46 campaigns share the identical $50.00 daily budget figure, almost entirely the Demand Gen batch tests, likely spun up from the same template.

Bid strategy hierarchy

Every one of the 68 campaigns with a recorded bid strategy runs its own standalone strategy. None are pooled into a portfolio bid strategy.

  • 52 run Maximize Conversions
  • 10 run Target ROAS
  • 4 run Manual CPC
  • 2 run Target CPA

Several campaign names track a tROAS ladder that implies graduation over time, for example "tROAS - 2.5x>2x>1.8x>1.4x>1.8x>1.5x." But the Bid Strategy Type field for most of these still reads Maximize Conversions, not Target ROAS. The name documents a progression the account hasn't actually made. With no portfolio strategies anywhere, each bid strategy learns from only its own, often thin, conversion volume. The same fragmentation visible at the campaign level (Section 01, Section 03) repeats one level down inside Smart Bidding.

Recommendation: Evaluate portfolio strategies only for campaigns that are eligible and share aligned goals and sufficient conversion volume. Pooling is not automatically superior, and not every campaign type supports it. Assess campaign type, conversion objective, target compatibility, conversion volume, and budget ownership before consolidating.

Tracking and measurement

Enhanced conversions match rate

Confirmed finding. Google Ads flags the primary purchase action for an Enhanced Conversions setup issue.

  • 11 conversion actions total: 7 rated Excellent, 4 flagged Needs attention.
  • "Google Shopping App Purchase (1)," the primary action, is flagged Needs attention for an Enhanced Conversions setup issue. The exact cause has not been confirmed from this data; possible contributing factors visible in the diagnostic panel include order ID, OAuth credentials, or hashed user data, but these are hypotheses, not a confirmed diagnosis.
  • Three secondary tag-based actions (View Item, Add To Cart, Page View) are missing user-provided data on 30 to 95 percent of events.
  • Offline conversion import is the exception: active and healthy, no action needed.

Recommendation: Review the diagnostic details and implementation ownership for the Purchase action. Confirm data quality before changing bidding. Signal quality on automated bid strategies may be affected; this data does not show that every bid strategy in the account is materially degraded.

Consent Mode v2 and the Restricted Targeting policy

Two separate issues here, and the second is the single biggest structural problem in the account.

  • Default consent labels tag data as not granted. Restricted data processing for the 20 US privacy-law states is switched off, covering 46.9% of total spend ($156,148), California alone 6.4%.
  • The Consent Mode tag itself works and reports "Excellent" for Web. This is a defaults and policy question, not a broken integration.
  • Every system-defined remarketing audience is closed under a confirmed Restricted Targeting in Personalized Advertising policy citation, tied to the product's health-adjacent, children's-focus content.
  • Treat it as a policy question, not a bug. Full detail in Section 07, Section 08, and Section 10.

Cross-campaign query-routing overlap

Confirmed finding. 6,394 of 30,914 distinct search terms, 20.7% of distinct search terms, triggered ads in more than one campaign. This figure is a count of distinct terms, not spend-weighted or impression-weighted overlap. 2,911 terms span 3 or more campaigns at once. Core terms like "saffron gummies," "saffron for adhd," and "best saffron for adhd" each trigger ads across 9 separate campaigns simultaneously.

Kids Theme, Mood Theme, ADHD Theme, and Gummies & Supplement Theme routinely route the same query to more than one campaign at once. This may indicate that each campaign's Smart Bidding strategy learns from an overlapping pool instead of a distinct one, on top of the fragmentation already flagged in Section 03 and Section 09.

Recommendation: Measure overlapping-query spend to size the overlap in dollar terms, not just term count. Compare routed campaign performance for the overlapping terms. Define campaign intent ownership for each theme. Add negatives only where overlap is confirmed unwanted, and preserve useful routing flexibility elsewhere.

Negative list architecture

Well organized for internal separation. Not built to catch the account's biggest external threat.

  • The two lists work in opposite, complementary directions: non-brand campaigns exclude brand terms, the Brand campaign excludes generic category terms. That's the correct structure, not a gap.
  • What it can't do, and doesn't attempt to do, is stop Amazon or an affiliate site from bidding on those same brand terms externally. That's addressed in Section 11 and Section 12's Branding keywords switch.
  • Worth adding a third list: a standing Category Drift list (hemp gummies, mushroom gummies, bergamot gummies, and similar), so this cleanup happens once, centrally, instead of per-campaign.

Performance Max

Asset group and search theme structure

Structure is good where it exists. One live campaign has none of it.

  • 7 asset groups total, across the account's 2 enabled PMax campaigns.
  • "Different LPs" runs 5, split by messaging angle: 10 Reasons ADHD, 7 Reasons Why, Natural Alternative Broad, Natural Alternative Search Themes, Natural Alternative Parents. Segmenting by angle, not by duplication, is the right approach.
  • "Different LPs" also has 35 search themes, covering the saffron, kids, and ADHD query space thoroughly, including "smartkidz saffron gummies," a direct competitor's name used to conquest. SmartKidz shows up in this account's own Shopping Auction Insights.
  • "Bed Wetting Angle" runs 2 asset groups (Broad, Monster Match) and has zero search themes. Nothing is steering it toward the bed-wetting query space. That may explain why bedwetting-brand domains (Goodnites, Ninjamas) showed up next to this account's auction insights in the Competitive Brief.

Search themes are additive signals for PMax. This may indicate that they do not restrict matching the way negative keywords or brand exclusions do.

Recommendation: Add search themes to "Bed Wetting Angle" at the same specificity already used on "Different LPs," as an additive guidance signal. Use negative keywords for control over unrelated bedwetting-brand queries, and brand exclusions where appropriate. Monitor Search Term Insights afterward to see whether delivery shifts.

Brand protection and search visibility

Brand is protected internally. What PMax actually matches on is not visible.

  • The shared brand list is applied as a Negative exclusion on 2 campaigns, keeps PMax's non-brand campaigns from bidding against the account's own brand terms.
  • PMax search-term visibility is more limited than Search reporting. Standard account exports don't include a PMax search terms report.

Recommendation: Review search terms where available, Search Term Insights, search categories, account-level negatives, and brand exclusions, on a standing monthly basis in the UI, not a one-time look. This may surface irrelevant or competitor query activity that account exports alone don't show.

New customer bidding

Data limitation. New-customer reporting requires validation before any bidding change. The weighted new-customer count is larger than total conversions for the full period, this mismatch needs an explanation before the figure is used to set a bid value.

  • 27,016 weighted new customers are tracked across the account, against 9,651 total conversions for the same full period (Section 01). Google Ads cannot confirm from this data alone why the weighted new-customer figure exceeds total conversions.
  • New Customer Lifetime Value is $0.00 on every conversion action.

Recommendation: Review Google Ads customer-acquisition settings, the existing-customer list definition, and possible conversion duplication before setting a bid value. Confirm what the weighted-customer figure actually measures. Do not assign an arbitrary New Customer Lifetime Value without this validation.

AI Max and auto-created assets

A deliberate, conservative setting, not an oversight, but worth testing now.

  • AI Max: Disabled on every campaign.
  • Final URL expansion: Disabled on every campaign.

Recommendation: Run a small-budget, time-boxed test with AI Max enabled on one lower-risk non-brand campaign. Expected result: a real read on whether AI Max's expanded targeting and asset generation help here, without risking the account's better-performing campaigns.

Data exclusions check

No data exclusion window is currently configured. Low spend alone does not justify a data exclusion.

Demand Gen

Confirmed finding. Section 01 already established that Demand Gen shows persistently low attributed efficiency during the observed period (0.45x lifetime attributed ROAS, 0.27x last 30 days). This data cannot confirm whether Demand Gen creates value outside what Google Ads measures, incremental or assisted effects are outside what this data can show. This section looks at contributing factors visible in Google Ads.

Audience signal quality

77.6% of the tagged view of Demand Gen delivery falls into "People not in audiences." That means it served users who matched none of the campaigns' configured audience signals.

Where a real audience segment IS matched, results are meaningfully better:

  • Beauty & Wellness: 2.07x
  • Family-Focused: 2.16x
  • Parents of Teens: 2.61x
  • Childcare & Education: 2.16x to 2.92x

The exception is "Converted Search Terms," the one segment that functions like a remarketing signal. It's the worst performer at 0.40x.

Optimized Targeting is turned on for 52 of the account's ad groups and campaigns. This setting lets Google show ads to people outside the audiences that were actually selected. Without strong audience signals to guide it (Section 04's policy restriction), the system explores broadly and mostly reaches people who don't match any set audience. Results are worse, though not a total loss.

Recommendation: Turn off Optimized Targeting on Demand Gen campaigns until real audience signals exist, either first-party lists once the policy restriction is resolved, or strong in-market and affinity signals in the meantime. This data suggests delivery would narrow to people more likely to convert, moving attributed ROAS toward the 2.0x-plus range already seen when a real audience segment is matched; this is not guaranteed. More broadly for the channel: reduce exposure while testing, separate testing budgets from scaled spend, define clear attributed-ROAS success thresholds before testing, test creative and targeting variables against those thresholds, and stop tests that miss them.

Creative diversity

  • 60 distinct video IDs referenced across 11-plus named creative batches and angles: 10 Reasons ADHD, 7 Reasons ADHD, Natural Alternative, PDP, Quiz Funnel, Bed Wetting.

Recommendation: Move from batch testing to a continuous testing loop, current industry best practice for video-heavy Demand Gen accounts. It works in three steps:

  • Score before launch. Run every new video against Google's ABCD framework (Attention, Branding, Connection, Direction) before it goes live. This catches weak creative before it spends any budget.
  • Test in batches. Run several scored videos against each other at the same time, not one after another. This is a multivariable test, it finds a real winner faster than sequential testing does.
  • Promote and repeat. Keep the winner running as the champion, retire the rest, and feed new challenger creative into the loop against it on an ongoing basis.

Expected result: fewer weak videos reach paid spend, winners get identified and scaled faster, and the channel keeps improving on its own instead of waiting for a quarterly review.

Personalized audience options

The Branding keywords switch

This is the account's proposed first automation asset: an hourly check against Auction Insights that reacts when Amazon or an affiliate domain starts overlapping or outranking the Brand campaign, adjusting bids or campaign status in response. It directly targets the leakage already confirmed in the Competitive Brief and Section 11. It would also double as the account's first piece of always-on monitoring.

arba

A separate tool with a separate purpose. Of the Google Marketing Solutions open-source tools available, arba diagnoses Quality Score-driven impression share loss, directly relevant to the DSA finding in Section 05. It has nothing to do with brand defense. The two would sit alongside each other, not overlap.
